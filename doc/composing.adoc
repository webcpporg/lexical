[#lexical-composing]
== Composing rules

Every format but `regex` is also a Boost.URL rule, in
`webcpp::lexical::grammar`: an object whose type `<name>_rule_t` is public,
so a rule that takes a parameter, `time_rule_t{leap_seconds::refused}` or
`iri_rule_t{&resource}`, is spelled without a factory. A rule yields the
text it matched, as a `grammar::text`; `uri_rule` and `uri_reference_rule`
yield Boost.URL's `url_view`. `ipv4` and `ipv6` are Boost.URL's own rules,
`boost::urls::ipv4_address_rule` and `ipv6_address_rule`.

A rule composes with Boost.URL's combinators, `tuple_rule`, `variant_rule`,
`optional_rule`, `range_rule`, into a grammar of a program's own: an
internationalized host and an optional port, `bücher.example:8080`, are a
`tuple_rule` of `idn_hostname_rule` and an `optional_rule` of another
`tuple_rule`, a colon, `squelch(delim_rule(':'))`, then
`unsigned_rule<std::uint16_t>`, which yields the host's text and the port's
number; a rule that refuses says whose rule it was, through its error's
category:

Where a rule stops inside a longer input is part of its contract:

[%header,cols="2,5"]
|===
| Rule | Stops
| the ASCII rules | where their grammar does
| `hostname_rule` | at the first character outside letters, digits, `-` and `.`
| `idn_hostname_rule` | at the first ASCII character outside letters, digits, `-` and `.`; every non-ASCII byte belongs to the name, since UTS 46 may map it
| `idna_label_rule` | as `idn_hostname_rule`, and also at `.`, U+3002, U+FF0E and U+FF61
| `email_rule`, `idn_email_rule` | where the domain or the address literal does
| `iri_rule`, `iri_reference_rule` | at the first byte no IRI holds: an ASCII control, the space, the double quote, the less-than and greater-than signs, the backslash, the circumflex, the grave accent, a brace or the vertical bar
| `uri_template_rule` | at the first byte that is neither a literal nor the start of an expression; an expression or a percent sign that starts and breaks makes it fail
| `json_pointer_rule` | at the end of the input: a token holds every other character
|===

Each validates the text it took and fails as a whole rather than match a
shorter prefix, and a rule that fails leaves its input where it was.
`idna_label_rule` is a terminal of lexical's own that asks `webcpp::idna` for
its verdict, so a grammar joins labels with separators of its own. It judges
each label alone: a grammar that joins labels into a name checks what spans
the name itself, RFC 5893's Bidi rule across its labels (section 2) and its
253 octets in the ASCII form, or hands the whole name to
`idn_hostname_rule`, which checks both. Joined by a grammar's own dots,
`0a.` and a label of a Hebrew letter are two valid labels and no valid name.

An internationalized rule's error is idna's, the `idna::error` that refused
the name (<<lexical-errors>>), but only where nothing discards it: Boost.URL's
`variant_rule` answers `mismatch` when every alternative fails, whatever
each one said. lexical's e-mail rules look for `[` themselves rather than
make the domain an alternative, so that idna's reason reaches the caller; a
grammar that needs that reason does the same.

Two facts of Boost.URL shape a composition. A `range_rule` over a rule
larger than 128 bytes keeps it in a pool of Boost.URL's own, which
allocates: pass such a rule through `boost::urls::grammar::ref`, as lexical
does. And `parse` of a whole string fails with `error::leftover` when the
rule stops early, which is how a validator refuses trailing input.
